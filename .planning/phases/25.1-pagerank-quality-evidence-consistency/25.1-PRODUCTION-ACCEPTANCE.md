Acceptance nevertheless failed because scheduler replay was not a fixed point.
Historical full crawls selected newer trusted crawls as comparison baselines,
so their evaluation revisions depended on each other and changed every minute.
Each replayed trusted full crawl then initialized Current Snapshot, moving the
project pointer successively from the newest crawl to older crawls. Production
evidence showed repeated applied events for the newest crawl, its predecessor,
and the original baseline in one scheduler pass.

The deployment was rolled back through a fresh `CHECK_ONLY=1` gate with no
active crawl. Only the app image was restored. The running app image is again
`sha256:96836ffd674b88312af4beaf8754de2e16695511f20599563a1bb032dde585c9`;
`/api/health` is `ok`; ClickHouse remained healthy and was not restarted. No
session or historical quality/evidence row was deleted or rewritten.

## Additional release gates

- A quality baseline is the latest trusted full crawl strictly older than the
  evaluated session by `(started_at, session_id)`; future sessions are never a
  baseline and the oldest session has no predecessor.
- Current Snapshot persists an authoritative source crawl and content watermark.
  A historical full or delta replay cannot replace or mutate content newer than
  that watermark, including after fold, restart, or ClickHouse compaction.
- Equal crawl timestamps use session ID as a deterministic tie-breaker.
- Repeated scheduler passes converge: after predecessor revisions settle, a
  further pass publishes no evaluation, promotion, or snapshot revision.
- Production acceptance must observe the same Current Snapshot source and
  revision across at least two scheduler intervals after the exact-session
  repair.
- Recent logs must contain neither promotion-loop warnings nor repeated applied
  promotions for superseded historical sessions.
